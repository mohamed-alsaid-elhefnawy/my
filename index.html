Scientific Foundations

Quantum Basics

Core principles of subatomic physics where continuous Newtonian mechanics yields to discrete probability waves.

Pillar 01

Energy Quantization

Energy is not continuous. In 1900, Max Planck discovered that atomic oscillators exchange energy in discrete packets called quanta, directly proportional to frequency.

$$E = h\nu = \hbar\omega$$
\(h = 6.626 \times 10^{-34}\ \text{J}\cdot\text{s}\) (Planck's Constant)
Pillar 02

Wave-Particle Duality

Subatomic entities propagate through space as extended phase waves, yet register as localized discrete particles upon measurement. Louis de Broglie showed every moving mass possesses a wavelength.

$$\lambda = \frac{h}{p} = \frac{h}{mv}$$
Validated by Davisson-Germer electron diffraction
Pillar 03

Born Probability Rule

The wavefunction \(\Psi(\vec{r}, t)\) is not an electric charge density, but a complex probability amplitude. Max Born proved that observable probability equals the squared absolute magnitude.

$$P(\vec{r}) = |\Psi(\vec{r}, t)|^2$$
Normalization: \(\int |\Psi|^2 dV = 1\) (Certainty)
Pillar 04

Quantum Superposition

Prior to measurement, a quantum system occupies a linear combination of all its basis states simultaneously. Measurement collapses the state vector into a single definite eigenstate.

$$|\psi\rangle = \alpha |0\rangle + \beta |1\rangle$$
Constraint: \(|\alpha|^2 + |\beta|^2 = 1\) (Bloch Sphere)

1. The Hydrogen Atom Problem

Hydrogen consists of one proton of mass \(M\) and one electron of mass \(m_e\) bound by Coulomb attraction. It represents the singular realistic atomic system whose non-relativistic time-independent Schrödinger equation possesses an exact analytical closed-form solution:

$$\left[-\frac{\hbar^2}{2\mu}\nabla^2 - \frac{e^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0 r}\right] \psi(r,\theta,\phi) = E \psi(r,\theta,\phi)$$

2. Probability Density Clouds

Heisenberg's Uncertainty Principle strictly forbids well-defined planetary orbital trajectories \(\mathbf{r}(t)\). Instead, Max Born's statistical interpretation dictates that \(|\psi(r,\theta,\phi)|^2 dV\) yields the probability of detecting the electron within volume element \(dV\):

$$P(V) = \iiint_V |\psi(\mathbf{r})|^2 d^3\mathbf{r}, \quad \iiint_{\mathbb{R}^3} |\psi|^2 d^3\mathbf{r} = 1$$

3. Separation of Variables

Due to spherical central potential symmetry \(V(r)\), the 3D Laplacian separates into radial and angular spherical harmonic components:

$$\psi_{nlm}(r,\theta,\phi) = R_{nl}(r) \cdot Y_l^m(\theta,\phi)$$

The radial wavefunctions \(R_{nl}(r)\) are expressed in terms of Associated Laguerre polynomials \(L_{n-l-1}^{2l+1}\), and angular factors \(Y_l^m\) in Associated Legendre polynomials \(P_l^m(\cos\theta)\).

4. Nodal Surfaces & Topology

Nodal surfaces are geometric regions where the electron wavefunction vanishes identically (\(\psi = 0\)), producing zero detection probability:

$$N_{\text{radial}} = n - l - 1, \quad N_{\text{angular}} = l, \quad N_{\text{total}} = n - 1$$

For example, the \(3d\) orbital has \(n=3, l=2 \implies 0\) radial nodes and \(2\) angular nodal cones/planes.

5. Quantum Numbers & Symmetry

Three spatial integers govern the state: Principal \(n \ge 1\) specifies energy; Azimuthal \(0 \le l \le n-1\) specifies orbital angular momentum \(L = \sqrt{l(l+1)}\hbar\); and Magnetic \(-l \le m \le +l\) determines spatial projection \(L_z = m\hbar\). Spin \(m_s = \pm 1/2\) provides intrinsic magnetic moment.

6. Bound Energy & Spectroscopy

In non-relativistic quantum mechanics, the hydrogen bound state energies depend exclusively upon principal quantum number \(n\), creating \(n^2\)-fold degeneracy (ignoring spin):

$$E_n = -\frac{\mu e^4}{32 \pi^2 \epsilon_0^2 \hbar^2} \frac{1}{n^2} = -\frac{13.606\ \text{eV}}{n^2}$$

Electric dipole selection rules \(\Delta l = \pm 1\) and \(\Delta m = 0, \pm 1\) regulate allowed radiative transitions.

The Quantum Measurement Paradox

Wave-Particle Duality

Subatomic entities propagate through both slits simultaneously as probability waves, yet collapse into localized particle hits upon detection.

Simulation Mode:
Accumulated: 0 hits
De Broglie Wavelength (\(\lambda\)): 35 nm
Slit Separation Distance (\(d\)): 45 μm
The Central Mystery of Quantum Mechanics: In Mode 1, continuous phase interference creates alternating constructive bright fringes and destructive dark fringes. In Mode 2, even when particles are fired one by one with days between shots, each particle passes through both slits in superposition, building up the interference fringes over time. In Mode 3, placing a detector destroys phase coherence, collapsing the wavefunction into two classical bands!

De Broglie Wavelength Equation

In 1924, Louis de Broglie hypothesized that any moving particle with momentum \(p = mv\) possesses an intrinsic matter wavelength:

$$\lambda = \frac{h}{p} = \frac{h}{m \cdot v}$$

For a 0.15 kg baseball traveling at 40 m/s, \(\lambda \sim 10^{-34}\ \text{m}\), far smaller than any atomic nucleus, making wave behavior unobservable. For electrons (\(m_e \approx 9.1 \times 10^{-31}\ \text{kg}\)), \(\lambda\) matches crystal lattice spacing, enabling electron microscopes.

The Fundamental Law of Quantum Motion

The Schrödinger Equation

Formulated in 1926 by Erwin Schrödinger, this partial differential equation governs the non-relativistic deterministic time evolution of a quantum wavefunction.

Time-Dependent Formulation (TDSE)
$$i\hbar \frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Psi(\vec{r}, t) = \hat{H}\Psi(\vec{r}, t) = \left[ -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\nabla^2 + V(\vec{r}, t) \right] \Psi(\vec{r}, t)$$
$$i\hbar \frac{\partial}{\partial t}$$

Energy Operator

Governs how the quantum phase advances forward in time: \(\hat{E} = i\hbar \frac{\partial}{\partial t}\).

$$\Psi(\vec{r}, t)$$

State Wavefunction

Complex amplitude in Hilbert space containing all physical information about the particle.

$$-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\nabla^2$$

Kinetic Energy (\(\hat{T}\))

Derived from momentum squared \(\frac{\hat{p}^2}{2m}\) with spatial Laplacian curvature \(\nabla^2\).

$$V(\vec{r}, t)$$

Potential Field (\(\hat{V}\))

External potential barrier, electrostatic Coulomb attraction, or harmonic oscillator well.
